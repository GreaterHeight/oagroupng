How to Recover Lost Revenue: A Practical Guide for Nigerian Businesses

Revenue leakage is rarely one problem. A disciplined recovery programme begins by finding where value is being lost, why it is happening and what can be changed.

By OA Group Editorial

Revenue leakage can hide in pricing, billing, collections, procurement, inventory, contracts or process handoffs. Recovery begins by treating the problem as a system rather than assuming that one overdue invoice explains the loss.

A practical diagnostic starts with the revenue chain: what should have been earned, what was billed, what was collected and where the variance occurred. Segmenting the variance by customer, product, location, contract or process can reveal patterns that a single aggregate figure hides.

The next question is causation. Some leakage is caused by weak controls; some by commercial terms; some by operational friction; and some by deliberate behaviour. Each cause requires a different response. Recovery work is strongest when financial analysis is combined with process review and management accountability.

A recovery programme should also protect future revenue. Fixing a disputed invoice without changing the underlying approval or reconciliation process simply moves the problem forward.
